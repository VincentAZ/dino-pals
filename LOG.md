# Dino Pals — Build Log

Autonomous build log. Newest entries at the bottom.

## 2026-10-20 — Kickoff
- Request: dinosaur web game for a 3-year-old, iPad (Safari) touch, deploy on Vercel, high-quality realistic Blender assets, full autonomy, keep a log.
- Environment: Blender 5.2.1 LTS (OPTIX/CUDA on RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell), Node 22.23, Vercel CLI authed as `vincentaz` (team `ai-learning-center`), gh authed as `VincentAZ`, Playwright Chromium available.
- Design decision: NO rules, NO scoring, NO failure. A 3-year-old needs cause-and-effect toys, not a game with rules. The app is a "dinosaur sandbox": big dinosaurs on screen, tap one and it roars/stomps/eats; drag to feed; swipe to pan. Every touch does something delightful, nothing is ever "wrong". Short name: **Dino Pals**.
- Tech decision: Vite + three.js, static site (Vercel static hosting, zero server). glTF/GLB assets with Draco/meshopt compression for fast iPad load. Audio via Web Audio unlocked on first tap (iOS requirement). PWA manifest so it can be added to the iPad home screen full-screen.
- Asset decision: Blender pipeline. Two tracks evaluated in parallel: (A) curate CC-licensed rigged+animated dinosaur models from Objaverse and clean/optimize/re-light them in Blender; (B) build dinosaurs procedurally in Blender. Pick best-looking per species.
- User directive (mid-build): "vast and expansive and diverse open world". Scope raised: free-roam 3D world with multiple biomes (jungle, volcano, river/beach, plains, swamp, snowy highlands), day/night cycle, weather accents, many dinosaur species, ambient life (butterflies, birds/pterosaurs, fish). Toddler constraints stay: single-thumb walking, tap-to-interact, no menus, no reading, no failure. Performance target: 60fps on iPad (A12+), <40MB initial payload with streamed world chunks.

## 2026-10-20 — Blender rig/animation pipeline (blender/dino_pipeline.py)
- Built a fully scripted, headless pipeline: import → junk removal (Sketchfab icospheres, rock bases, ground planes) → bake foreign rigs at their first frame → join → auto-orientation (long axis + triangle-density "head is denser" heuristic) → real-world scale → ground/feet origin → decimate to 20–50k tris → procedural armature fitted to the mesh (spine curve from per-slice medians, legs from XY clustering of low vertices with adaptive height cut and bimodal splitting, jaw, wings, flippers) → gaussian distance skin weights + smoothing → procedural keyframed clips (idle, walk, roar, eat | idle, fly, roar | idle, swim, roar) → GLB export with WebP textures → preview renders of every clip from two angles for visual QA.
- Bugs found by looking at renders (not by trusting logs): glTF importer leaves objects in QUATERNION rotation mode so `rotation_euler` was silently ignored (Triceratops & Sauropelta faced backwards; fixed); stale EditBone references after mode switch; Blender 5 layered actions (no `action.fcurves`); REST-pose baking of Sketchfab rigs produced vertical meshes (bake the posed first frame instead); merged leg clusters on low-slung species (adaptive z-cut + forced split).
- All 15 species verified facing +Z, standing on y=0, with working idle/walk/roar/eat (or fly/swim). Known cosmetic: faint seam at Ankylosaurus neck during roar; Spinosaurus (modern low-slung reconstruction) walks a bit stiffly.
- Final asset pass: forced non-metallic materials (Sketchfab metallic maps render as black patches), cleared custom split normals on Triceratops, disabled weight smoothing on Ankylosaurus (fixed the neck seam). Post-processed with gltf-transform (dedup, prune, 1K cap, Draco edgebreaker): 15 GLBs = 15.8 MB total (0.4–3.7 MB each), all validate clean. `web/public/assets/dinos/manifest.json` generated with per-species attribution (CC-BY) and measured heights.

## 2026-10-21 — Foot sliding fix (user feedback: "dinos walk very unnaturally, almost like they are sliding")
- Cause: walk playback rate was relative to a hand-typed species speed, unrelated to the clip's real stride, and only set once per state change; legs barely articulated.
- Blender: walk clip now swings thighs ±30°, folds the knee and lifts/plants the foot; the pipeline measures each clip's foot excursion (stride per cycle) and re-times the clip so stride ÷ duration equals the intended walking speed per species (T. rex 1.3 m/s, sauropod 1.0, raptor 1.2 …). Found and fixed a leg-tagging bug (two legs sharing a tag pooled their samples). Stride and clip length are written into the manifest.
- Engine: every frame, walk timeScale = ground speed ÷ natural speed (so feet cover exactly the ground distance, including follow mode and scaled individuals); blends walk↔idle by speed so starts, stops and in-place turns no longer moonwalk; footstep sounds follow the clip's half-cycle.

## 2026-10-21 — Locomotion + feeding rework (user feedback: sliding when rotating, "wonky" walk, bad food pile, misaligned eater, no eating feedback)
- Turning: dinosaurs now pivot in place in the idle pose (no stepping → no sliding) when the target is far off-axis, and otherwise arc with a yaw rate limited by speed ÷ turn radius; speed ramps with size-dependent acceleration and slows into stops.
- Walk clip: removed the body roll, lighter hip yaw, slight forward lean, smaller bob on big species.
- Feeding: each eater walks to its own stand point one snout-length from the pile (snout distance measured per species in Blender and shipped in the manifest), pivots to face it, then eats; up to 3 eaters per pile at different sides; a separation step keeps dinosaurs from overlapping. The eat clip pitch is solved per species so the snout actually reaches the ground (scan-based solver; bisection diverged on the Ankylosaurus).
- Food pile is a real Blender asset (Poly Haven fern fronds with alpha + fruit), 3 m across, that shrinks and loses fruit as it is eaten; each chew emits a crunch and a leaf puff at the snout; a heart puff and sparkle when it is finished. Eating takes ~35 s alone, ~12 s with three eaters.
- Verified by probe screenshots: eaters line up with heads on the pile; smoke suite passes.

## 2026-10-21 (night) — Locomotion rebuild (user feedback: "still wonky", "slides when rotating", "walks through trees and each other", food fake and not reached)
User asked for a great game, no shortcuts. Unreal was considered and rejected: Unreal has no web export any more (HTML5 removed in 4.24) and Pixel Streaming needs paid GPU servers per player — not viable for a free iPad web app on Vercel. The problems were in rig/animation/AI, not the engine.

**Root cause found by rendering the skeletons:** the v1 auto-rig was wrong (spine dipping into the belly or climbing into plates/sails, legs mis-placed, quadruped knees bending backwards). No animation can fix that.

- **Rig v2 (blender/rig_v2.py):** mesh voxelised into a solid (surface sampling + exterior flood fill); spine = medial axis (largest inscribed circle per cross-section, tracked with continuity); legs traced upward from ground contacts through the solid until they merge into the body, ankle/knee fitted to the traced centre line (3-segment least squares), ball + toe joints from the foot's ground contact (digitigrade feet), raised feet found for posed models, girdles symmetrised; mouth line from the jaw gap; end-effector bones (toe.*, snout); geodesic skin weights through the solid (leg bones seeded only below where the limb leaves the body, wider blends at hips/knees). Deformation stress-tested at extreme strides for every species.
- **Model fixes found by the films:** Triceratops duplicate low-poly inner shell removed (caused hanging strands) and flipped faces → all dino materials double-sided + forced opaque; Carnotaurus replaced with the same artist's neutral-pose model (the old one stood with a foot on a removed rock).
- **Procedural locomotion (web/src/dinos/locomotion.ts):** feet planted in world space, two-bone IK + foot/toe aiming, gait clock driven by real ground speed incl. turning in place (biped L/R, quadruped lateral-sequence walk), predictive foot placement, settle steps when stopping, pelvis bob/sway over the stance leg, pelvis reach solver (lowers hips so no foot is ever over-stretched), body tilt from feet on terrain, tail lag on turns, head look-at (the child), neck CCD reach for eating, footstep sounds + dust from actual foot plants.
- **Steering (ai_land.ts + path.ts):** sampled velocity obstacles (time-to-collision against trunks, canopies for tall animals, rocks, other dinosaurs as 2-circle bodies, explorer and camera), keep-right preference + priority yielding, grid A* route planning per body size with string pulling, re-plan on stall, hard non-overlap constraints as a backstop; stepping pivots instead of instant snaps when tapped.
- **Measured (tests/gait.mjs, tests/collide.mjs):** stance foot slip ≤ 1.2 cm and IK reach error ≤ 5 cm on all 12 land species (sauropod was 46 cm before the reach solver); zero dinosaur–tree and zero dinosaur–dinosaur overlaps in straight-at-a-tree runs, a 5-way crowd crossing and 90 s of free roaming.

## 2026-10-21 (night, cont.) — Feeding + gait realism
- **Bugs found by measuring, not guessing:** (1) three.js AnimationMixer only writes a bone when its value changes, so restoring the bind pose each frame erased every held clip pose (eat/roar heads popped back up) — now the last clip pose is restored instead; (2) aliased `slerpQuaternions(IDENT, q, w)` in the IK helpers zeroed every weighted rotation; (3) unbounded CCD could fold the head back under the body — per-joint total caps; (4) snout bone was the most-forward vertex (a horn on Triceratops) — now the lower front of the head on the centre line; (5) head look-at stayed on while eating; (6) the old food asset in blender/out was overwriting the new one in postprocess (removed).
- **Eating:** each eater commits to one item, stands one measured eat-reach back from it, pivots onto it, the jaw snaps shut on that item (it flies into the mouth), head lifts and chews with sound; shuffle steps between bites; per-animal reach trim learned from real bites; two eaters per pile (others wait and watch); co-eaters may bring heads together. Result: 24 bites/pile, median 4 cm, p90 12 cm from the item.
- **Food pile:** 24 photoscanned Poly Haven items (bananas, apples, pomegranates, pears, avocados, citrus, sweet potatoes) settled with rigid-body physics on a fern/leaf nest (blender/food/build_food.py), shown at 1.5× so it reads from the child's camera; dropped 5.5 m in front of the child.
- **Gait timing from biomechanics:** stride length from Alexander's dynamic similarity (λ/h = 2.3·Fr^0.3), walk→trot transition at Froude 0.25 with quadrupeds blending lateral-sequence walk into diagonal trot, foot homes under the hips (models are posed mid-stride). Hungry herbivores trot to food. Final 12-species measurements: stance slip ≤ 1.6 cm (walk), IK reach ≤ 20 cm worst-case.
- Triceratops: inner shell kept (it fills holes in the perforated outer skin) and given the skin's weights, so it deforms with the skin.
- **Interaction polish:** tapping a dinosaur from afar makes it walk over to say hello and stop a friendly distance away facing the child; hold-to-follow now walks beside the child, ahead and in camera view, instead of behind the camera; carnivores walk up to a food pile, sniff it and wander off (they never eat it); the pterosaur and sea reptiles were verified on film (needed: apply the test camera before frustum culling).
- **iPad performance:** route planning rewritten (binary-heap A*, terrain on a coarse lattice, capped search, 1 plan per frame): worst case 86 ms → 5 ms per plan. Distant dinosaurs update their animation at 30 Hz (45–100 m) / 15 Hz (beyond). Each dinosaur now ships a 20 % LOD mesh on the same skeleton, used beyond ~30 m + 1.5× its length. Under Chrome CPU throttling ×6 with the food scene: 51 fps, 1.53 M triangles (was 3.2 M).
- **Real animal voices:** dinosaur calls are now built from public-domain/CC0 recordings on Wikimedia Commons (audio/build_voices.py): alligator bellows (big theropods — the classic film-dinosaur basis), an elephant trumpet slowed to a deep hoot (sauropods), bison grunt + cow moo (horned/armoured/ornithopods), great blue heron + peacock (raptors), turkey gobble (Pachycephalosaurus), sandhill crane + gull (pterosaur), humpback blows (sea reptiles). Calls are cut by silence detection, pitched per animal size, band-limited, faded, loudness-normalised (-16 LUFS); the engine varies pitch ±6 % per call and adds a soft sub-bass swell under the big voices. Ambient birds per biome are real recordings too (NPS Yellowstone library, gulls, kookaburra, cockatoos, loons). 1.1 MB total, MP3 for iOS. Synth voices remain as fallback. Credits listed in the parent "i" screen.
- **Skin detail:** tileable reptile-scale normal map (blender/textures/make_scales.py, wrapped Worley noise, domed scales) blended UDN-style into every dinosaur skin's own normal map at a real-world scale (UV density measured per species in the pipeline), fading out by ~2.5× body length. Verified with an A/B at 0×/1×/1.6× (found and fixed: onBeforeCompile sees unexpanded #includes).
- **Memory bug fixed:** every dinosaur instance used to parse its own GLB (duplicate textures/geometry per individual — 182 distinct dino textures once all were loaded, ≈0.5 GB of GPU memory on iPad, a likely Safari tab crash during longer play). Now one parse per species, individuals are SkeletonUtils clones sharing geometry/materials/textures (79 textures); placeholder geometry/materials are disposed when the real model arrives (geometries 890 → 288).
- **Families:** Triceratops, Stegosaurus, Paluxysaurus, T. rex, Iguanodon, Ankylosaurus and Pachycephalosaurus bring a baby (0.38× / 0.25× scale) that walks beside its parent (setting off together with it), grazes when the parent eats and answers when the parent calls (higher-pitched voice). Speeds scale with √size (dynamic similarity) so babies keep up. Idle herbivores graze the ground now and then (head down, leaf puffs, soft crunch).
- **Safari engine check:** Playwright WebKit (WPE, the engine behind iPad Safari; the two missing gamepad/HID libraries were unpacked into Playwright's own browser folder, nothing installed system-wide) runs the full game: WebGL2, MP3 decoding, tap → roar, food → eating, 0 errors (`web/tests/webkit_smoke.mjs`).

### iPad lifecycle hardening
- **WebGL context loss** (Safari drops contexts under memory pressure or after long backgrounding): three.js re-uploads on restore; if the context does not come back within 4 s the page reloads instead of freezing. Tested by forcing `WEBGL_lose_context`: the restored frame matches the original (mean luminance 107.6 vs 108.6), taps still work, and a permanent loss reloads to the splash.
- **Audio interruptions** (phone call, Siri, another app): any touch now revives a non-running AudioContext.
- **Screen wake lock** is re-acquired after the OS releases it on hide (the old code kept a stale handle and never asked again).
- New test: `tests/lifecycle.mjs`.

### Toddler-proofing taps (measured with `tests/spam.mjs`)
- **Problem:** a toddler taps fast. Every tap restarted the action, so a Triceratops or raptor tapped 12 times in 3 s sat frozen on the first frame of its clip, and the first roar was cut off after 0.27 s. Taps on a T. rex fired a stomp thump per tap.
- **Fix:** taps on a dinosaur now alternate roar and its second action (stomp, sniff, graze, swoop, splash). A tap that lands while a tapped action is still in its first 75% queues the next action instead of restarting, with a small twinkle at the head so the tap still registers. Each action now plays to the end, then the next begins. Tapped actions also push back the dinosaur's own unprompted roar, so it no longer roars twice in a row.
- Credits panel closes when the dark backdrop is tapped.

### Toddler monkey test (`tests/monkey.mjs`)
- 5 minutes of real CDP touch input: random taps, taps and tap-spam on dinosaurs, drags, pinches, 3-6 finger palm mashes with partial lifts, long holds, food and magic button spam, info-button taps.
- Result: 330 actions, 0 errors, all positions and camera values finite, 0 dinosaur-dinosaur or dinosaur-tree overlaps, fps never below 60 on the dev GPU, food piles capped at 4, GPU geometries 235 to 423 and textures 191 to 327 as new areas streamed in (bounded), and a dinosaur still roars on a tap at the end.

### Rendering cost fixes (found with the new `probeTris` hook and `tests/tris.mjs`)
- **Velociraptor cornea used glass transmission.** Whenever a raptor was on screen, three.js rendered the entire opaque scene a second time into a transmission target. With a crowd in view the frame went from 1.61M to 2.77M triangles and 530 to 985 draw calls. The cornea is now a thin dark glossy alpha coat. Transparent eye coats draw in a single pass.
- **Dinosaur meshes skipped frustum culling.** Each skinned mesh now carries a padded rest-pose bounding sphere. `tests/cull.mjs` spins the camera through 16 views of a crowd with culling on and off: the only differing pixels are the animated loading dots, never a dinosaur.
- **Contact-shadow blob** for distant dinosaurs is now a soft radial falloff instead of a hard-edged disc.
- **Flat physical skins.** The T. rex, Paluxysaurus and Ankylosaurus exports carried Blender's "Specular = 0" as glTF IOR 1000 with a zero or tiny specular colour. That means no highlight at all: the skin looked chalky and the eyes had no glint, and the physical shader is the heaviest one on an iPad. These skins now load as standard PBR (F0 0.04, like real skin). An A/B film (`?rawmats` keeps the old materials) shows the same look with a little natural sheen.

### Adaptive resolution governor
- iPads differ a lot, and any iPad slows as it warms up over a long session. The startup probe only ran once.
- Every 2 s the governor takes the **median** frame time (robust to garbage-collection and shader-compile spikes), ignoring tab switches and the first 3 s after streaming settles. Below about 45 fps it lowers render scale in 15% steps to 0.7, then drops to the low tier. When frames hold vsync for 10 s it sharpens again. If an upgrade causes slowdown within 10 s, upgrades lock out for 90 s, so it can't oscillate.
- `tests/governor.mjs` emulates a slow device: it starts sharp on high, trades resolution, drops to low, then recovers scale 0.7 to 1.0 once the load goes away. Tests that force a tier with `?q=` keep it fixed unless they pass `&gov=1`.

### Smaller fixes
- **Loading dots** in the top-right stayed on forever in the meadow. The condition double-counted loaded dinosaurs and waited for 25 of 50, but only about 21 are ever in loading range. They now mean "something near you is still arriving".
- **Flaky connections:** a failed dinosaur download used to stay cached as a failure, leaving that species grey all session. It now retries after 12 to 20 s, up to three times.
- **Stale taps:** a queued tap action expires after 6 s, so it can never fire late after the dinosaur wanders off.
- **Startup quality probe removed.** It averaged the first 3 s after the world appeared, which are full of streaming spikes. A fast iPad could be dropped to the low tier for the whole session, and nothing ever upgraded it back. The tests never caught this because they force the tier. The governor now handles startup too: if the very first steady-state reading is below 33 fps on high it goes straight to low. `tests/governor.mjs` also loads the real production URL and checks that a fast machine stays on high at full sharpness.
- **Route planning hitches.** Profiling split the planner into grid, search and smoothing. Grid building was 94% of the time: every route sampled walkability, about 9 µs of procedural noise per sample (`scripts/bench-terrain.mjs`), on ground nobody had visited yet. Walkability is now memoised (1 m grid for steering, world-aligned 4 m grid for routes), and the 4 m grid is warmed outward from the explorer at 0.6 ms per frame. At six-times CPU throttle the worst route went from 29 ms to 2.7 ms, total route time from 149 ms to 25 ms, and the frame rate from 35 to 47 fps. Collision and feeding tests are unchanged (zero overlaps, median bite 3.9 cm).
- **Deprecations:** DRACOLoader `setDecoderConfig` removed (WebAssembly is the default). Shadows use PCF explicitly (PCFSoft was removed in three r18x and already mapped to PCF). WebKit now loads with zero warnings from our code.
- **Grown-up tips** in the info panel and README: Add to Home Screen for full screen, Guided Access to keep the child in the app.


## 2026-10-21 — Environment realism pass (grass, trees, terrain, volcano, light)
Brief: the owner wants "detailed assets throughout" and a world that "looks real". Baseline review of the start view: sparse single grass blades on a flat-coloured ground, broadleaf trees and bushes reading as "cotton balls" of composed leaf cards, a smooth speckled volcano cone, low-detail terrain, flat light. Dinosaurs / food were out of scope (parallel work).
- **QA first:** `tests/envqa.mjs` renders 18 fixed views (start, two grass close-ups, every biome, volcano from meadow / slope / crater, four times of day) for both tiers in one browser session, logging `stats()`, a WebGL texture-allocation tally and the bytes fetched before the world is shown; `tests/envsheet.py` builds contact / before-after sheets; `tests/treeqa.mjs` and `tests/viewer.html` photograph vegetation. Baseline captured before any change (`tests/shots/env/before`).
- **Grass (world/grass.ts, world/field.ts):** clumps of curved, tapered blade geometry (no alpha test; Lambert + wrap + translucency, receives shadows) in two toroidal rings around the player (dense 3-segment near ring, sparse 1-triangle far ring) plus a wildflower ring; frustum-culled in 4x4 blocks. Colour, coverage and blade type (meadow grass with dry blades and lying thatch, swamp reeds, straw tufts that grow as whole clumps) come from a shared "ground field" that the terrain shader evaluates as well — same splat weights, same tileable macro noise — so the grass fades into ground of exactly its own colour. Nothing grows on snow, fresh volcanic ground or cliffs. The Poly Haven grass-clump species were retired.
- **Trees and bushes (scripts/foliage.mjs):** every modelled Poly Haven leaf (20-30 triangles) becomes one textured quad fitted to it (least-squares UV→position map); an outer-weighted subset is kept per LOD and enlarged to keep crown coverage; normals are bent to the crown ellipsoid and crown AO baked into vertex colours; real limbs are kept. Applied to the three island trees, jacaranda, tree_small_02 and three shrubs. In the engine foliage keeps its normal on both faces, gets transmitted light when backlit and keeps alpha coverage across mip levels. Vegetation LODs are now bucketed on the CPU (each LOD batch holds only the instances inside its distance band; parts share one instance buffer), LOD1 trees cast shadows except in dense forest, and impostors are rendered into albedo + normal atlases and shaded with the live sun/sky. Conifer needle cards were brightened.
- **Terrain (world/terrain.ts, heightmap.ts, biomes.ts):** 15 Poly Haven layers (new: rippled `aerial_sand`, wet `brown_mud_03`, `brown_mud_leaves_01`, volcanic `rock_ground_02`, layered `cliff_side`) packed as albedo+height and roughness/normal/AO maps, uploaded straight through GL (alpha carries data). Shader: four strongest splat layers height-blended, biplanar cliff projection on steep ground (grey rock / red sandstone / basalt by region), `floor_pebbles_01` grit detail near the camera, two-scale macro variation and anti-tiling, wet glossy swamp mud with puddles, rusty scoria on the volcano. The worker bakes horizon ambient occlusion and contact occlusion under tree crowns and around rocks into a vertex attribute (deterministic neighbour placements, cached). 512 WebP layers load first; 1024 layers stream in afterwards as Basis/KTX2 into a GPU-compressed array.
- **Volcano:** heightfield with warped radial gullies and spurs, ridged rock at two scales, old-flow lobes, five levee-banked lava channels from the rim and a crater lava lake; lava is shaded as flowing crust plates over a hot core with a temperature ramp (deep red → orange → yellow-white) and a warm glow on its banks.

### Dinosaur and food textures GPU-compressed (KTX2)
- **Problem:** the environment agent's memory tally showed dinosaur and food models expanding to hundreds of MB of RGBA8 textures. Each 1024 texture costs 5.6 MB with mipmaps, and the Carnotaurus alone had 20, including full 1024 sets for claws, eyes and teeth. On an iPad with 3 to 4 GB of RAM, Safari can kill the tab.
- **Fix** (`web/scripts/compress-dino-textures.mjs`, now also the last step of `blender/postprocess.sh`):
  - Colour and roughness/AO become ETC1S. Normal maps become UASTC with rate-distortion tuning and zstd, since ETC1S ruins normals. On the iPad these transcode to ASTC/ETC2 and stay compressed in GPU memory.
  - The dead "Specular 0" extensions and their textures are stripped.
  - Textures used only by parts under 3% of a model's triangles (eyes, claws, teeth, horns, tongues) drop to 256 px. Food textures are capped at 512 px.
  - Geometry is re-encoded with the pipeline's exact Draco settings.

### Stale-asset caching bug (may have affected what the iPad showed)
- `vercel.json` has served everything under `/assets/` as `immutable, max-age=1 year` since the first commit. That is right for Vite's content-hashed bundles. It is wrong for the data files in `public/assets`: dinosaur models, the dinosaur manifest, food, environment models, ground textures and audio keep the same names between deploys. Once Safari had cached them it could keep using old models and rigs with new code for a year. That may be part of why earlier fixes looked only "a little better" on the iPad.
- Changing the header cannot evict what Safari already holds, so the URLs now change instead. `vite.config.ts` hashes every file in `public/assets` at build time, and `src/assets.ts` appends `?v=<hash>` to every data URL through the three.js loading manager and a `fetch` wrapper. A deploy re-downloads exactly the files whose content changed, and immutable caching stays safe. `tests/cachebust.mjs` fails if any data request goes out without its hash.

### Motion smoothness pass (what made walking still look "wonky")
Foot-slip numbers were already good, so I added a per-frame motion recorder (`motionSample` hook, `tests/smooth.mjs`, report via `tests/smooth_report.py`). It samples every nearby dinosaur every frame for 30 s in three scenarios (free roaming, food dropped, rapid taps) and flags jolts, hip dips and sideways sliding. It found four real causes:
1. **Hip dips.** When a leg could not reach its planted foot (turning in place, slopes), the hip solver dropped the hips at a rate of 30/s, which is effectively instant: up to 0.66 m in 0.2 s on the sauropod, a third of its hip height on a baby. Now any planted leg pulled past 93% of its length steps right away, as animals do, and the remaining hip correction is rate-limited to 0.45 leg lengths per second. Turning slip also improved: Paluxysaurus 10.8 to 1.2 cm, Triceratops 4.3 to 1.7 cm.
2. **Dead stops.** `brake` and `pivotToward` lowered the speed value but never moved the body, so animals froze from walking pace in a single frame. Tapped, echo-roaring and grazing animals stopped the same way. They now glide to a halt (`coast`), checking ahead for animals, trees and the explorer and camera.
3. **Sideways sliding.** Steering modelled each body as two circles at about half its length, so a sauropod's neck end was invisible to it until the overlap fix shoved the whole body sideways at up to 2.2 m/s. Bodies are now three circles covering the full capsule. Turning in place also sweeps a long body like a door; a turn that would push further into another animal or a tree is now held back (`turnBlocked`), and babies stand outside their parent's turning circle.
4. **Feeding shuffle.** It switched direction and speed in one frame and flickered between stepping and turning at its 10 cm tolerance. It now has its own acceleration-limited velocity, settles out on arrival, and holds "arrived" until it drifts well past the tolerance.

| Measured, worst over all nearby animals | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Hip vertical speed | 8.8 m/s | 1.8 m/s |
| Body acceleration, single frame | 26 to 50 m/s² | 3 to 10 m/s² |
| Body acceleration, 100 ms window | n/a | 3.3 m/s² |
| Sideways slide speed | 2.2 m/s | 0.5 m/s |

Regression after the pass: gait slip 1.1 cm or less, zero overlaps in the collision suite and in a 200 s monkey run, feeding 23 bites with a median 8.7 cm from mouth to item, smoke, WebKit, air/water, family and sleep all clean.
